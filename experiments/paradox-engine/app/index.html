The Paradox Engine

Four instruments that break your own reasoning, in your browser. Nothing here uses an AI, nothing leaves your machine, and every verdict is computed, not guessed.

Socratic Interrogator

State a belief. The interrogator asks why, then asks why again, until your justifications run out — then it shows you what the structure actually looks like: circular, contradictory, or resting on an unexamined assumption. It never tells you whether you are right; it only examines whether your reasoning holds together.

Fallacy Foundry

Paste an argument. The foundry scans it for the shapes of twelve classic fallacies — cue phrases and argument skeletons — and flags them inline, with a note on what to check. It is a detector, not a judge: your text never leaves this page, and the final call is yours.

Self-Reference Lab

Build a small universe of sentences that talk about each other's truth, and watch the engine try to decide what is true. Some systems settle; some never can. The engine uses the same fixed-point semantics logicians use for the liar paradox — and it will happily show you the exact sentence (or chain of sentences) that breaks your universe.

Preference Paradox

Rank things the honest way — one head-to-head at a time. When you finish, the lab builds your preference graph and checks whether it contradicts itself. It can. You can genuinely prefer A over B, B over C, and C over A, all at once. Most people find out about their own taste cycles this way.

Which do you prefer?

Runs entirely in your browser — zero dependencies, zero network requests. The verdicts are computed by four small engines; their tests live next to them in this repository.
